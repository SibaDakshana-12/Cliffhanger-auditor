# Problem proof: weak episode endings

## (a) What I watched

| Source | Show type | How it ended | Cliffhanger? | Wanted next? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kuku TV episode | Microdrama: a company head is treated rudely by a guard | Ended on a cliffhanger | Yes | Yes |
| Kuku TV episode | Microdrama: a kidnapper demands money from a boyfriend | Ended on a cliffhanger | Yes | Yes |
| Instagram reel 1 | A rich boy's romantic collision at college | A hook, but no cliffhanger | No | No |
| Instagram reel 2 | An emotional woman signs her husband's property papers | The story simply continued; I found it boring | No | No |

Ending types I saw: withheld reveal, humiliation / status reversal, threat or
ransom, a romantic hook with no cliffhanger, and a flat continuation with no pull (reel 2).

## (b) Reviews and comments
I didn't read app-store reviews. Instead, I read comments on Instagram channels that post short-form AI videos.

| Source | Date | One-line summary | About endings, or paywalls/coins? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Instagram comments on channels posting short AI videos | not recorded | Viewers are very interested, and most comments ask for the next part. When a video doesn't end on a cliffhanger, people make fun of it instead. | Endings |

No screenshots were saved, so this is my own observation, not documented evidence.

## (c) Viewer input: friends described
- 3-4 friends described reels where the story stops just before a hero's hidden power or identity is revealed, often after he is insulted.
- Friends said suspense endings and unique stories keep them watching, and cliffhangers excite them most of the time.
- Friends said poor animation or audio loses them, and stories of the same type lose their interest.

## (d) Who has this problem and how I know
- **The person this tool is for:** the editor or story reviewer who checks whether each episode ends well. (Assumption: I haven't confirmed how Kuku's editors work today.)
- **Who feels it:** viewers. In the short videos I've watched, people really wait for the next part when a video ends on a cliffhanger. Otherwise they just make fun of the video. The two Kuku episodes I watched ended on cliffhangers and made me want the next one. Instagram reel 2 just continued, and I found it boring.

## (e) Why this matters more than Problem 1 and Problem 2, including the overlap with Problem 1
- **Problem 1** picks promo moments from finished episodes. My tool finds weak endings while they can still be fixed.
- **Problem 2** (AI lawyer) has nothing to do with Kuku's business. My problem is tied directly to Kuku's own product: episodes that make people want the next one.
- **Overlap with Problem 1:** a good promo also has to end on a cliffhanger, so the same ending judgment could help with promos too.

## (f) Limits
- The sample is small: 2 Kuku episodes, 2 Instagram reels, 3-4 friends.
- Endings are one factor among several. My friends also named animation, audio and story variety. This doesn't show that endings alone decide whether people keep watching, and it doesn't generalise to all viewers.
- Not verified, only assumed: (a) editors currently check endings by hand; (b) re-cutting or re-ordering existing material is possible in their workflow.
